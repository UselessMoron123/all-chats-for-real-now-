Ah, excellent scholarly curiosity before plunging into mud-slinging destiny! Let’s talk “peasant habitats” in the medieval world.  

Strictly speaking, peasants were *the* backbone of agrarian societies all across Europe (and elsewhere), so their “natural habitat” was… farmland, villages, and the lord’s ever-watchful tax collector. But some countries are especially iconic in the collective imagination:

---

### 🌾 Western & Central Europe
- **England:**  
  Probably the most famous “peasant setting” in pop culture. Think yeomen in muddy fields, serfs owing labor to their lords, Robin Hood’s friends stirring rebellion in the green forests. By the later Middle Ages, England had a lot of *tenant farmers* (peasants renting land rather than bound serfs), plus that very famous **Peasants’ Revolt of 1381**.  
  *Pros:* Relatively dynamic economy, some room for upward mobility (if you lived past age 35).  
  *Cons:* The Black Death might carry you off, and poll taxes could spark a revolution you’ll get squashed in.

- **France:**  
  A vast peasant population, very stratified, and a rural heartland famous for wine and bread. French serfs in some areas gained freedom earlier than in places like Russia, enjoying more tenant-farmer opportunities.  
  *Pros:* Fertile land, some vibrant markets to sell your wares, cursed but delicious cheese.  
  *Cons:* Endless wars (looking at you, Hundred Years’ War), and armies loved trampling your wheat more than fighting each other.  

- **The Holy Roman Empire (Germany-ish):**  
  A patchwork of territories, each with its own customs. Peasants could sometimes find more negotiating power, since so many little lords contested land ownership and wanted to attract laborers.  
  *Pros:* If you’re savvy, you can leverage competing lords trying to “poach” peasants.  
  *Cons:* Taxes of *amazing variety*—tithe for the Church, rent for the lord, fees for the miller, tolls for crossing a stream, etc.  

---

### 🌍 Eastern Europe
- **Russia:**  
  Peasants here lived in the mir (village communes). Compared to Western Europe, serfdom lasted *much longer*. In medieval Russia, peasants were heavily tied to the land, and over time the system got stricter, binding them nearly as property.  
  *Pros:* Vast open spaces, your whole village tight-knit, vodka as cultural glue.  
  *Cons:* Heavy, enduring serfdom. Fewer routes to freedom than in Western Europe.

- **Poland/Lithuania/Bohemia (Central & Eastern lands):**  
  Options varied a lot, but overall peasants here became increasingly constrained by lords during the later medieval era.  

---

### ✨ Outliers for “Peasant Advantage”
- **Scandinavia (Norway, Sweden, Denmark):**  
  Unique! Many peasants here were *free farmers* rather than serfs. They often owned their land, owed lighter dues, and even had a say in local assemblies (things like the “thing” system).  
  *Pros:* Cold winters but high autonomy, decent place for a sturdy peasant to thrive.  
  *Cons:* Soil can be harsh, winters bite hard, Vikings may recruit you as cannon fodder.  

- **Southern Italy & Iberia:**  
  Varied dramatically, but some regions had peasants with mixed opportunities due to feudal blending with older Roman and Islamic systems. Farmers might find themselves in more commercialized economies near ports.

---

### In Short
- **Most Iconic in Storytelling:** England/France  
- **Most Opportunities for Rising up (relatively):** Scandinavia (freer peasants), pockets of Germany (competition among lords)  
- **Most Oppressive:** Russia (with long-lasting bonded serfdom)  
- **Most Likely to Star in a tragic-yet-heroic folk ballad while starving:** France or England, circa the Hundred Years’ War.  

---

So, in LitRPG terms:  

- **England** is the *Balanced Start Zone* (lots of drama, rebellion quests).  
- **France** is the *Hard Mode* (constant wars, delicious food buffs).  
- **Scandinavia** is the *Peasant with Perks DLC Expansion* (extra autonomy, frozen landscapes).  
- **Russia** is the *Survival Grind Mode* (endless toil, but grind XP in resilience is very high).

Ohhh, this is fun: you’re basically asking me to map out “where peasants live in pop culture vs. history”—and this is where things get juicy. Let’s break it down:

---

### 🏰 **Monty Python (esp. *Holy Grail*)**
- **Setting:** A parody-"England," but really it’s a pastiche of generic medieval Europe. They filmed much of *Holy Grail* in Scotland (Doune Castle, anyone?), but the world is meant to be “Arthurian Britain” filtered through absurdism.  
- **Peasants in Pythonverse:** Famously argumentative! Notably the “autonomous collective” mud-farming serfs who shout about anarcho-syndicalism while King Arthur stands around awkwardly. These peasants are iconic precisely because they highlight the absurdity of romanticizing the Middle Ages.  
- **Takeaway:** Think “satirical England.” While rooted in the cultural myth of gloomy British mud huts, it’s intentionally cartoonish.  

---

### ⚔️ **Kingdom Come: Deliverance**
- **Setting:** VERY grounded. It takes place in **Bohemia (modern-day Czech Republic)** around 1403, during the civil war after the death of Charles IV of Luxembourg.  
- **Peasants here:** Mostly tenant farmers, villagers bound under feudal obligations, living in a very detailed and historically authentic rural countryside. The game is famous for its authenticity: survival depends on eating, sleeping, repairing clothes... and peasants aren’t background—they *are* you. You begin as the son of a blacksmith, not some chosen knight.  
- **Takeaway:** This is “Central European Sandbox Mode” for peasants—authentic, detailed, and gritty, not glorified.  

---

### 🐉 **China (Medieval context)**  
Now, here’s where things diverge dramatically from the “European peasant model.”  
- Instead of feudal serfdom, peasants in **Imperial China** were technically “free,” but they owed taxes, corvée (forced public labor on projects like the Great Wall), and were heavily structured into the bureaucratic imperial system.  
- Most people were small **subsistence farmers** tied to village communities. Landlords (gentry class) often monopolized fertile land, pushing peasants into tenancy.  
- BUT—there was a unique *avenue out of peasantry*: the **imperial examination system.** While incredibly difficult and reliant on literacy (rare for peasants), in theory any male could sit for exams, pass, and enter the bureaucracy—vaulting from “plowboy” to “mandarin” status. This potential for upward mobility is very different from serfdom in, say, France or Russia.  
- **Takeaway:** Farming millet, rice, and tea instead of barley and turnips, answering to bureaucrats instead of lords, with slimmer but real chances to level up socially.  

---

### 🎭 Summary / in RPG terms
- **Monty Python peasants**: *Comedic Class Mod* — high in [Wit] stat, immune to [Authority Checks].  
- **Kingdom Come peasants**: *Hardcore Realism Expansion* — survival stats matter, everything’s historically accurate, no fireball spells (unless ale counts).  
- **Chinese peasants**: *Different Tech-Tree Branch* — bureaucracy replaces nobility, farming rice paddies instead of wheat fields, with potential “questline” of passing exams to escape peasantry (high INT stat requirement, brutal RNG).  

---

So if Western peasants were stuck grinding chores and running from marauding knights, Chinese peasants were grinding *rice taxes* and trying to dodge corvée labor… but with a narrow chance to “prestige class” into the mandarin scholar track.
