Ah, “North Korea mode” in **Crusader Kings II** — the most paradoxical of Paradox playstyles. It’s not an official feature, but rather a tongue‑in‑cheek nickname the player base gave to a very… *non‑feudal* way of running your realm. The name comes from the idea that the ruler holds absolutely everything directly with no vassals, mirroring the stereotype of extreme centralization.

Here’s the breakdown:  

---

### **What North Korea Mode Is**
- In CK2, you normally rely on vassals (counts, dukes, kings) to hold most of your realm because your **demesne limit** — the maximum number of holdings you can hold personally — is quite small.  
- “North Korea mode” ignores that entirely: you keep *all* counties, baronies, and cities in your direct control. No counts, no dukes, no feudal vassals — just you as the landlord of basically the entire map.  

---

### **How It Works**
- **Normally:** Go over your demesne limit → big penalties to income and levies from holdings → realm efficiency tanks.
- **With NK Mode:** You mitigate or outright ignore those penalties by stacking massive bonuses from stewardship, laws, and game exploits.  
- This typically involves:
  - Max stewardship via traits, education, spouse choice, and councilors.
  - Using Late Feudal law boosts.
  - Sometimes abusing game mechanics like certain succession exploits or retinues.
- You personally get the full benefit of every holding’s income and levies (proportionally penalized if you go over limit, but with enough stewardship, you can hold *a lot* before the penalty becomes crippling).

---

### **Advantages**
- Absolute control — no vassal revolts, no succession crises from vassals splitting off.
- All your levies are yours to command directly — instant army without vassal contribution drama.
- All taxes go directly to you — potentially insane treasury growth.
- Immensely satisfying for megalomaniac role‑playing.

---

### **Drawbacks**
- Painful micromanagement — every building, levy raise, and garrison is *yours*.
- If your ruler’s traits or circumstances change (e.g., a poor inheriting heir), your stewardship may collapse and throw you far over the demesne limit, gutting income and levy size.
- No vassals means no vassal opinion bonuses from handing out land, no help with internal politics.
- Technically an exploit — not how the game was balanced to be played — so some consider it a “cheese” strat rather than “legit.”

---

### **Why People Love Doing It**
Mostly for the novelty and power fantasy — the CK2 world is designed to simulate feudal politics, where angry vassals are half the fun and half the challenge. “North Korea mode” throws that entire layer out the window and turns CK2 into something closer to a straightforward empire‑management wargame. And sometimes, you just want to drop the medieval diplomacy drama and become the crowned CEO of Europe.

Got it — we’re moving from **“ruthless iron-fisted empire”** territory to something far more *zen garden*.  

**Playing tall** in Crusader Kings II is all about focusing on **quality over quantity** — you concentrate on building up a small, manageable realm instead of sprawling across half of Eurasia. Think “the wealthy and perfectly groomed duchy of your dreams” rather than “my flag is everywhere.”

---

## **The Core Idea**
Instead of conquering vast swathes of land (“playing wide”), you:
- Keep your realm compact, typically just your capital duchy and some choice counties.
- Invest heavily in buildings, technology, and infrastructure.
- Make your demesne incredibly rich, defensible, and advanced.
- Use your power to punch above your weight without risking the headaches of managing dozens of rebellious vassals.

The fantasy: You’re less Genghis Khan, more Florence under the Medici — famous, rich, and influential without ruling the map.

---

## **Why Play Tall**
- **Low internal drama** → fewer vassals means fewer revolts and factions.
- **Faster tech spread** → fewer counties means you can bring them all up to the cutting edge quickly.
- **Insane personal income** from fully upgraded holdings.
- **Elite military**: Small realm, big budget = top-tier retinues, mercs, and fortifications.
- **RP potential**: Perfect for building a cultural, religious, or dynastic legacy.

---

## **How to Do It Well**
1. **Choose the Right Start**
   - Pick a rich area with high base tax and good development potential (Constantinople, Venice, Paris, Flanders).
   - Coastal and trade-friendly locations are great for income.

2. **Keep Land Within Your Demesne**
   - Stay under or at your **demesne limit** to avoid efficiency penalties.
   - Focus on one duchy’s worth of counties.

3. **Upgrade Everything**
   - Build and upgrade castles, cities, and temples to max levels in your demesne.
   - Don’t forget tech buildings: Military Organization, Legalism, Economy advances.

4. **Money Is Power**
   - Fund mercenaries during wars instead of relying on large vassal levies.
   - Maintain retinues for instant-response armies.

5. **Diplomacy & Marriage**
   - Use alliances to deter aggression, since you lack sprawling vassal levies.
   - Marry smart for claims and non‑territorial advantages.

6. **Pick Supportive Laws**
   - Centralize for higher demesne limit.
   - Maximize city/town taxation if you invest in them.

---

## **Play Tall vs Play Wide**
|  | Play Tall | Play Wide |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | Small, compact | Vast, multi-region |
| **Focus** | Infrastructure & tech | Expansion & conquest |
| **Strengths** | Economy, tech, stable politics | Sheer numbers, territory control |
| **Weaknesses** | Limited manpower, risky if swarmed | Fragile politics, succession chaos |

---

## **The Secret Sauce**
Playing tall is the art of *being too expensive to attack and too well-connected to ignore*. You’re the medieval equivalent of that one small but unbelievably bougie neighborhood where every house has a Tesla in the driveway and you wonder, “What exactly do they *do* in there?”
