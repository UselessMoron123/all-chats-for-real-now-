Narrate. World's pov seeing one peculiar rising dynasty arranging marriages all around the globe. Their members equally intermarrying with prosperous families, and with fading ones. They don't seek conquest - in set of decisive actions (not only war), they carved their own domain once, and then saw no need to expand their Archduchy any further. They send their people searching for knowledge, geneology, myths, in paper, oral traditions, rumors. They accept people seeking better life (declining house, or these whose royal parents were overthrown). They could offer money and give better promises. They could buy the marriage if target has an unwilling parent. They don't bother with target not being in line of succession. They could even invite people seemingly not descending from any famous house (if origins long forgotten. or if they are cadet branch of cadet branch [x] times. or if their ancenstor was a bastard of someone actualy important, what often gets forcibly forgotten). They marry families with claims of descending to someone, be they acquired by diligent research, masterfully fabricated with money or cunning, or by their ancestor doing something fitting of figure of the past. Often they'd accept last members of some families. It's not rare for them to set up matrilineal marriages - invited men marrying their women, kids being of mothers dynasty. Or men marrying invited women, with kids of different dynasty - It doesn't bother them much if the child of these unions isn't of their dynasty, as long as it makes the bloodline continue, so it can be absorbed later. But in the end, even making marriages with someone seemingly insignificant, they still looked like they were the winners.

We watched them rise from the dust of minor nobility, carving out their Archduchy with a speed and ferocity that made empires tremble. We braced for the inevitable tide of expansion, the hunger for neighboring borders, the endless wars of painting the map in their color.

But then, the armies stopped. The borders calcified. They built high walls, not to keep us out, but to turn their domain into a fortress of solitude.

Then, the emissaries came. Not generals, but genealogists. Not conquerors, but couriers bearing chests of gold and heavy scrolls.

To the rest of the world, their behavior was madness. In the courts of Paris, Constantinople, Baghdad, and Delhi, we whispered about them. They played the Great Game, but they played it by rules written in invisible ink. They did not seek alliances for soldiers. They did not seek land for taxes.

They sought the Blood.

They hunted for ghosts. Their agents were found in dusty libraries and burned-out hamlets, tracing lineage like veins of gold in granite. They sought the descendants of dead empires and forgotten heroes. It became a joke in the taverns: *“If your great-grandfather polished the boots of a Saint, the Archduke wants to marry you.”*

But the joke died when we saw who they took.

They welcomed the wreckage of history. A prince whose father was overthrown and blinded? They gave him a palace and a wife from their own kin. A beggar woman who claimed descent from the old Merovingians, laughed out of every other court? They bathed her, dressed her in silk, and wed her to their heir.

They did not care for the present station of a person. They ignored the laws of succession that the rest of us hold sacred. It did not matter if the target was the seventh son of a seventh son, or a bastard born on the wrong side of the sheets, provided the blood of the father—the blood of the *legend*—coursed through them. If the records were lost, they paid scholars to find them. If the records did not exist, they paid scribes to forge them so masterfully that even God might be convinced.

Most baffling were their contracts. They defied the natural order of patriarchy. They would invite a man of illustrious heritage—a descendant of the Great Khans or the Caesars—and force him into a matrilineal union. The children would bear the mother’s name, the Archduke’s name. We thought it humiliating for the grooms, yet they went, seduced by safety, by gold, by the promise of a legacy preserved.

And when they could not demand the name, they compromised. They would marry their daughters to wandering exiles, allowing the children to bear the exile's name. We laughed, thinking they were diluting their dynasty, scattering their power to the winds. *“They are breeding themselves out of existence,”* we scoffed.

We were wrong. We were thinking in years; they were thinking in centuries.

They were banking the bloodlines. They let those foreign names persist for a generation, maybe two, keeping them close, keeping them safe within their court. And then, a generation later, a cousin would marry a cousin—distant enough to avoid the rot of madness, close enough to fold that precious, diverted river back into the main ocean of their dynasty.

They collected myths. They collected the strength of giants, the intellect of philosophers, the sanctity of miracle-workers. While we fought over strips of mud and border forts, they were conquering history.

Now, when we look at their current ruler, we do not just see a man. We feel the weight of the world. In his veins flows the blood of conquerors from the Steppes, Emperors of Rome, Prophets of the Desert, and Kings of the North.

They did not expand their borders, yet they conquered the world. For when you look into the eyes of their Archduke, you are looking into the eyes of every great man and woman who has ever lived. We hold the land, but they... they hold the soul of mankind.
