The obsession with "improving the stock," hunting for specific genetic traits, and long-term breeding programs is a recurring theme that bridges dark history, grand strategy gaming, and high-concept sci-fi.

Here are similar things categorized by how they manifest in the real world and in fiction:

---

### 1. Real-World History (The "Bloodline Hunting")

The CK2/CK3 player mindset actually has a strong historical precedent, though usually driven by power and land rather than "genetic traits."

*   **The Hapsburgs:** The ultimate real-life "bloodline hunters." They used strategic marriages to consolidate almost all of Europe under one family. However, like a CK2 player who takes it too far, they ended up with the "Hapsburg Jaw" and extreme genetic fragility due to centuries of inbreeding.
*   **The *Lebensborn* Program (Nazi Germany):** This was a state-supported, registered association in Nazi Germany with the goal of raising the birth rate of "Aryan" children via extramarital relations between "racially pure" women and SS officers. It is the darkest real-world parallel to a Bene Gesserit-style program.
*   **The Repository for Germinal Choice:** Founded in 1980, this was a sperm bank specifically for Nobel Prize winners and high-IQ individuals. It was an attempt at "positive eugenics," trying to breed a more intelligent generation. It closed in 1999.
*   **The Ghengis Khan Effect:** While not a "program," Genghis Khan is the most successful biological "bloodline" in history. Approximately 1 in 200 men worldwide carry his Y-chromosomal lineage, a result of conquest rather than a spreadsheet, but it represents the "spreading the trait" mechanic players love.

### 2. In Fiction (The "Bene Gesserit" Archetype)

Many stories feature "Grand Designs" where characters are treated as biological chess pieces.

*   **The Elder Blood (*The Witcher*):** Ciri’s lineage is the result of a massive, generations-long breeding program by the Elven Sages (Aen Saevherne). They were trying to create a "Child of the Elder Blood" to save the world, but their plans were derailed when a human fell in love with their "subject."
*   **The Targaryen Dynasty (*Game of Thrones*):** This is the "incest for traits" trope. They practiced sibling marriage to keep the "blood of the dragon" pure, believing it was the only way to maintain their bond with dragons. They deal with the "Coin Flip" (madness or greatness), a mechanic many CK2 players know well.
*   **The Genetic Dynasty (*Foundation* TV Series):** The Empire is ruled by three clones of the same man (Cleon I) at different ages. This is an attempt to freeze a bloodline in time forever to ensure stability, effectively "winning" the game of succession by never changing the player character.
*   **The Primarchs (*Warhammer 40,000*):** The Emperor of Mankind used his own genetic material to create 20 super-beings. This is "Eugenics via Lab" rather than breeding, but the obsession with "Gene-seed" purity and lineage among Space Marines mirrors the bloodline-hunting mechanic.

### 3. In Video Games (The "Min-Maxing" Genetics)

Beyond Crusader Kings, other games tap into the "Genetic Architect" itch:

*   **Fire Emblem: Genealogy of the Holy War:** This is the closest game to CK2. You must pair up units in the first half of the game specifically so their children in the second half inherit "Holy Blood" and powerful weapons. Players spend hours mapping out which "bloodlines" will result in the most broken units.
*   **Mass Effect (The Krogan/Genophage):** The Krogan are defined by their biology. Their entire culture is obsessed with "breeding rights" because their fertility was biologically suppressed. The Krogan warlord Wrex is effectively trying to play a high-stakes game of "bloodline consolidation" to save his race.
*   **Mass Effect (The Asari):** They are the "Bene Gesserit" of the galaxy. They live for 1,000 years and strategically mate with other species to "add" those species' best traits to their own culture, without actually changing their own DNA.

### 4. High-Tech/Future Concepts

*   **Gattaca:** A film about a "not-too-distant" future where eugenics is the norm. Parents choose the best genetic traits for their children (hair color, IQ, athletic ability), creating a "Valid" upper class and an "In-valid" underclass born through natural means.
*   **CRISPR and "Designer Babies":** In modern science, we are entering the era of "Genetic Engineering" rather than "Breeding." This allows for the selection of traits (initially to remove diseases) that mimics the CK2 player’s desire to remove the "Slow" or "Fragile" traits from their heirs.

### Summary: The Common Link
All these examples share the **"Instrumentalization of People."** Whether it’s a Bene Gesserit Sisterhood, a CK2 player, or a 17th-century King, they all stop seeing individuals as people and start seeing them as **data points/vessels** for a specific legacy or power.
