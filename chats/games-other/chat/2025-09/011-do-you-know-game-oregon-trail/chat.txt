Oh yes—the *Oregon Trail*! A true classic of gaming history. It first appeared in classrooms in the 1970s as an educational computer game, and over the years it’s been re-made many times but always with the same spirit: guiding a group of pioneers westward from Missouri to Oregon in the mid-1800s.  

The charm (and notoriety) of the game comes from its mix of resource management, survival challenges, and unpredictably grim outcomes. You’re in charge of a wagon party, and you need to:  

- **Choose supplies** at the start—everything from oxen to spare wagon wheels.  
- **Manage food, pace, and rest** while traveling thousands of miles along the trail.  
- **Cross rivers** that may or may not swallow up half your stuff.  
- **Hunt for food**, which somehow always devolved into shooting far more buffalo than four humans could ever eat before it spoiled.  
- **Deal with calamities** like accidental gunshot wounds, snake bites, and, of course, the infamous dysentery.  

It’s part strategy, part history lesson, part “wow, life in 1848 was rough.” And despite the blocky graphics and simple mechanics, it became deeply beloved—partly because so many kids played it on school computers, and partly because of that quirky blend of education and dark humor.
