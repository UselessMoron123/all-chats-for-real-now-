The air in the bustling market square of **DuLoc** was thick with the smell of overripe fruit, cheap perfume, and desperation. Wanted posters papered every available surface, fluttering in the breeze like morbid bunting. Most featured the same, grim-faced sketch of a wanted fairy tale creature.

You were there on business. Unpleasant business. The kind that involved a heavy coin purse and a whispered meeting with a man who smelled of turnips and deceit.

Your contact, a weaselly little man named **Boris**, had just slipped you a grimy parchment. "Third stable on the left, outside the west gate," he hissed, his eyes darting. "The beast is ready for transport. Half now, half upon delivery to the castle."

As you counted out the coins into his sweaty palm, a commotion erupted near the central fountain. The crowd parted like a wave before a ship, and through the gap strode **Lord Farquaad**, mounted on a comically large white horse to compensate for his… modest stature. His polished armor gleamed, and his expression was one of profound, pinched distaste.

"Let it be known!" his herald, a man with lungs like bellows, shouted. "By order of Lord Farquaad, this kingdom is hereby cleansed of magical vermin! Ogres, trolls, pixies, talking animals—all are to be relocated! A bounty of twenty silver pieces per head!"

A murmur ran through the crowd. Twenty silver was a fortune. Boris snatched his coins and vanished into an alley.

Suddenly, a small, frantic shape darted between your legs—a **talking gingerbread man**, missing a leg, his frosting smudged with dirt. "Run!" he squeaked, his voice a high-pitched warble of panic. "He's gonna dunk me in milk! The horror!"

Right behind him, two of Farquaad's armored guards clanked in pursuit, their movements awkward in the tight press of the market. One lunged, his gauntleted hand swiping just past your shoulder as the gingerbread man leapt onto a cart of cabbages.

The guard glared at you. "You! Citizen! Seize that confectionery criminal!"

All eyes in the immediate vicinity turned to you. Lord Farquaad, from his elevated perch, watched with cold, expectant interest. The gingerbread man looked up at you, his candy eyes wide with a mixture of fear and hope.

What do you do?
